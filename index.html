About the group

The P²-Group

We are a small group of chemists focusing on the electronic structure studies of novel main-group and transition-metal compounds using state-of-the-art techniques. Our main research direction includes pursuits of structure, chemical bonding, and reactivity.

The Theoretical & Computational Chemistry Group (P²-Group) is led by Prof. Parameswaran Pattiyil in the Department of Chemistry, National Institute of Technology Calicut, Kerala. We collaborate closely with experimental groups in India and abroad.

Research

Research interests

01

Coordination chemistry at the main-group elements

Bonding, stability and reactivity of main-group compounds, including low-valent and ligand-stabilised species.

02

Designing of ligands and catalysts

Computational design of ligands and catalysts, guided by an understanding of electronic structure.

03

Computational biomimetic catalysis

Quantum chemical modelling of biomimetic catalytic systems.

04

Computational nano-materials

Theoretical studies of nanomaterials and their hybrids.

Current research thrusts

A

Heuristic bonding models

Formulating unique bonding representations by investigating coordination at Group 14 tetrel elements, and dissecting the classical electron-sharing model versus donor–acceptor interactions using Natural Bond Orbital (NBO) population analyses.

B

Rule-breaking catalyst design

Designing novel organophosphorus ligands, pseudo-silapnictenes and silylene-stabilised systems that bypass rare transition metals, and exploring routes to selective molecular transformations and polymer degradation.

C

Heterobimetallic chemistry

Characterising novel metallocenes of the form [Cp–Be–M–Cp]+ (M = B, Al, Ga, In, Tl), mapping polarisation changes and hyperconjugative donations with energy decomposition schemes (EDA-NOCV).

Dr Parameswaran Pattiyil is a Professor at NIT Calicut whose research spans theoretical and computational inorganic chemistry, with particular emphasis on non-classical chemical bonding, unconventional molecular architectures, and the rational design of novel catalytic systems. His work combines advanced computational methodologies with fundamental chemical theory to understand and predict the structure, bonding and reactivity of molecular systems.

He earned his Ph.D. from the University of Hyderabad under the guidance of Prof. E. D. Jemmis. He then held international appointments as an Alexander von Humboldt Research Fellow at Philipps-Universität Marburg (with Prof. Gernot Frenking) and as a JST Postdoctoral Fellow at the Fukui Institute, Kyoto University (with Prof. Keiji Morokuma). In 2024 he was recognised as a Fellow of the Royal Society of Chemistry (FRSC).
